Cases 1–2 compare each genuine article against itself and serve as identity controls.

rewrites. Both removal controls detected the missing paragraph, including
a regression against an earlier shared-boilerplate false positive.

Notion pipeline. Case 4 is the existing narrower-scope Unboxing rewrite.
The 38.52% / 22.61% values are lexical distinctive-token presence only,
not percent of factual content retained. Semantic paraphrasing and intentional
safety corrections may appear unmapped. These cases require human review.

## Short source paragraphs are no longer invisible

A: 48 substantive units plus 36 separate short/heading records.
B: 33 substantive units plus 29 separate short/heading records.
Every original paragraph is accounted for with a stable ID and SHA-256.
Short or heading items are NOT automatically judged retained or dropped.

## Generalized V1.2 Skill change

- Reusable source-to-rewrite paragraph traceability and unique-detail matching;
  false-positive boilerplate disambiguation; numeric claim review candidates.
- Explicit machine states rather than a fictitious semantic-retention score.
- Private machine JSON report and manual review worksheet from every Codex
  or optional API rewrite output, without new model calls for QA.
- Reviewer decisions RETAINED, CHANGED_WITH_APPROVAL and
  INTENTIONALLY_OMITTED require source hashes, a reviewer and rationale.
- No human-fidelity PASS is inferred from lexical similarity alone.

## Limits and next Skill UAT

This proves a checker can locate exact copies and controlled omissions in
two different real article types. It does not prove the matcher has resolved
all real paraphrases. Source B has no corresponding real rewritten version
on record, so cross-article output-fidelity scoring is not yet complete.
True semantic retention and factual safety require human review.
Cross-brand/cross-industry UAT remains future work.
